Agenda 02

Multimodal Learning

Getting speech, text, images, and brainwaves to speak the same language.

  1. Speech–Text Multimodal Modeling for Psychosis and Suicide-Risk Assessment

    In progress · since Sep 2026

    Reading signs of psychosis and suicide risk from what people say and how they say it — together.

    We are building text + speech multimodal models for psychiatric assessment, using clinical interviews with people experiencing psychosis and crisis-line calls involving suicide risk. The models jointly learn from transcripts and audio, so linguistic and acoustic cues can inform each other rather than being read in isolation. The goal is tools that support, rather than replace, clinicians and crisis responders.

    The data is as sensitive as data gets, so privacy and careful evaluation come before anything else.

  2. Diffusion-Based Image–Audio Alignment

    Third author · with Biyang Xin, Mingwei Sun, Shijie Zhang, and Xitong Li

    In progress · since Aug 2026

    Diffusion models that learn what a picture sounds like, and what a sound looks like.

    A diffusion-based multimodal framework for learning aligned image–audio representations, enabling cross-modal correspondence and generation across visual and acoustic modalities. Trained on paired image–audio clips collected from YouTube.

    Code in Github. Public soon

  3. EEG-to-Speech Decoding via Cross-Modal Representation Learning

    First author · with Mingwei Sun, Shijie Zhang, and Xitong Li

    In progress since Apr 2026 · planned submission to ICML 2027

    Decoding speech content from EEG, evaluated across subjects and content so the model cannot cheat.

    An EEG-to-speech-content framework that maps variable-channel EEG signals to speech representations using coordinate-aware CNN–Conformer encoders.

    Leakage-controlled evaluation across subjects, speech content, and datasets, with counterfactual EEG controls to check that decoding reflects neural signals rather than memorization.

    Code in Github. Public soon

Agenda 04

Management Information Systems

Where the data meets the money.

  1. Content Similarity in Recent Reviews and Hotel Revenue

    Second author · with Baojun Gao and Jiawen Wang

    Manuscript in preparation · planned submission to International Journal of Hospitality Management

    Do hotels pay a price when their recent reviews all start to sound the same? 1.5 million TripAdvisor reviews and revenue data from 2,990 hotels across Texas cities.

    Preliminary analyses suggest a negative association between adjacent review similarity and hotel revenue, varying by hotel segment, review volume, and rating.
